# General purchase strategy — social proof first, expert review second

## Purpose

Use this workflow for phones and other consumer products. The guiding principle is **social proof first, then expert validation**—but never confuse popularity with quality.

## Step 1: Translate the requirement into a testable checklist

Separate requirements into:

- **Must-have:** failure means reject the product.
- **Strong preference:** useful, but negotiable.
- **Nice-to-have:** use only as a tie-breaker.
- **Accessory solution:** solve outside the product where practical.

For this phone purchase:

- Must-have: India warranty, trustworthy seller, stable brand/support, budget ceiling.
- Strong preference: eSIM, good travel connectivity, durable body, long software support.
- Nice-to-have: microSD expansion, premium built-in cover glass, very high benchmark score.
- Accessory solution: tempered-glass protector and protective case.

## Step 2: Check social proof before expert reviews

For each candidate, collect:

1. Average user rating.
2. Number of ratings and number of written reviews as separate fields (platforms such as Flipkart expose both).
3. Recent review trend, if visible.
4. Number sold or bought recently, when the marketplace exposes it.
5. Seller identity, warranty, and return terms.

### Popularity rules

- Prefer **4.2+ stars with at least 500 ratings** for a mainstream phone.
- Treat **4.0–4.19** as acceptable only when there are at least 1,000 ratings and the product is otherwise a strong fit.
- Treat fewer than 200 ratings as weak evidence, even if the average is 4.5.
- Never compare raw star ratings across unrelated categories; compare phones with phones, chargers with chargers, and so on.
- Weight rating count and written-review count together. A 4.3 rating from 12,000 ratings and 1,000 written reviews is stronger social proof than 4.6 from 40 ratings. Do not merge Amazon rating counts and Flipkart rating/review counts without recording the platform.
- Look for recurring negatives: heating, battery degradation, network drops, camera fogging, green-line/display problems, service-centre difficulty, and seller/warranty complaints.

### Simple social-proof score

Use a 10-point social-proof score:

- 0–2 points: rating quality
- 0–3 points: review volume
- 0–2 points: recent sales/popularity signal
- 0–2 points: seller/warranty confidence
- 0–1 point: category-level consistency across Amazon/Flipkart

A candidate should normally score at least 7/10 before expert-review research begins.

## Step 3: Validate the product page and exact SKU

Confirm:

- exact RAM/storage and colour variant;
- Indian model number;
- eSIM support for that exact regional model;
- physical SIM and microSD arrangement;
- IP rating and warranty exclusions;
- seller, invoice, return/replacement policy;
- whether the price depends on exchange, bank discount, coupon, or a third-party seller.

Do not infer eSIM from an overseas review. Do not infer microSD support from a generic family name.

## Step 4: Use expert reviews to explain the rating

After social proof clears the threshold, consult at least two independent sources, ideally:

- GSMArena for battery, display, camera, thermal and charging measurements;
- Notebookcheck for display, performance, battery and build testing;
- 91mobiles, Gadgets 360, Beebom, or reputable Indian reviews for India-specific network, camera, service and value context.

Manufacturer benchmark claims can be noted, but they should not be treated as independent evidence. Prefer sustained performance, battery runtime, throttling, display accuracy, camera consistency, and update/service history over a single benchmark number.

## Step 5: Apply a weighted decision score

Suggested weights for a phone:

- 25% social proof and reliability signals
- 20% software/support/service confidence
- 15% durability and repairability
- 15% battery and thermal behaviour
- 10% travel connectivity/eSIM
- 10% camera/display quality
- 5% price/value

If a true must-have fails, reject the product regardless of score.

## Step 6: Solve with accessories where sensible

A phone does not need factory Gorilla Glass to accept a good external screen protector. For this purchase:

- budget about **₹250–₹600** for a good tempered-glass protector;
- budget about **₹500–₹1,500** for a case with raised bezels and corner protection;
- verify that the protector is made for the exact display curvature and fingerprint sensor;
- remember that a protector prevents scratches and helps with minor impacts, but it does not make the phone drop-proof or water-proof.

This means built-in Gorilla Glass should be treated as a durability bonus, not an absolute requirement.

## Step 7: Make a price-tier recommendation

- **₹20,000–₹25,000:** strong value and social proof; eSIM and premium durability are usually compromises.
- **₹28,000–₹35,000:** better balance of build, performance and popularity; still check eSIM by SKU.
- **₹38,000–₹50,000:** realistic range for a highly popular, well-supported phone with eSIM and a strong IP rating, such as Galaxy S24 FE or Pixel 9a-class devices, subject to exact India specifications and current pricing.

## Step 8: Recheck shortly before purchase

Marketplace prices, sellers, stock, reviews and bundles change. Recheck the final candidate on the purchase day, and save a screenshot or PDF of the listing showing model number, price, seller and warranty.
