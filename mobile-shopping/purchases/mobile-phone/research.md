## Shortlist observations

### 1. Motorola Edge 60 Fusion, 8 GB/256 GB variant

- Amazon search exposed an 8 GB/256 GB listing around **₹20,900**, while other listings and the official Motorola page were around ₹28,999–₹30,000 or higher. This is a large price discrepancy; seller, exact SKU, warranty, and return terms must be checked.
- Flipkart describes the model as 12 GB/256 GB, Dimensity 7400, 5500 mAh, 68 W charging, and expandable storage up to 1 TB. Because that expansion claim conflicts with some other published specifications, it must be confirmed from the exact India SKU/tray before relying on it.
- Motorola's official page states IP68/IP69 water and dust resistance and MIL-STD-810H testing. Motorola explicitly says laboratory durability testing is not a guarantee against future damage.
- Strongest physical-durability candidate, but the curved display and unclear eSIM/microSD status make it a conditional recommendation.

### 2. Samsung Galaxy F36 5G, 6 GB/128 GB or 8 GB/128 GB

- Amazon search showed approximately **₹22,400** for 6 GB/128 GB and approximately **₹23,776–₹24,399** for 8 GB/128 GB, depending on colour/seller.
- Search results and product listings identify a 6.7-inch 120 Hz Super AMOLED panel, Exynos 1380, 5,000 mAh battery, and 50 MP OIS camera. Several Indian specification listings identify Gorilla Glass Victus+ and a microSD/SD installation path.
- Samsung software/service coverage and the mainstream Samsung ecosystem are positives for a stability-focused purchase.
- eSIM was not positively confirmed for the Indian F36 SKU. Treat it as **not eSIM-qualified unless the exact listing or Samsung support confirms it**.
- It lacks the explicit IP68/IP69 positioning of the Motorola candidate, so a case and screen protector remain important.

### 3. OnePlus Nord CE5

- OnePlus India lists a starting price of **₹24,999**.
- Official claims include Dimensity 8350 Apex, 7,100 mAh battery, 80 W charging, Sony LYT-600 with OIS, 120 Hz AMOLED, and six years of software support (four Android upgrades plus six years of security updates).
- OnePlus publishes an AnTuTu result of 1,474,030 from OnePlus Test Labs. This is a vendor-run result, not an impartial benchmark; it is useful directionally, not as a buying guarantee.
- Official page does not establish Gorilla Glass, eSIM, or microSD expansion. Do not buy this model expecting those features without checking the exact retail specification.
- Good value/performance and support story, but it is less aligned with the user's Gorilla Glass/eSIM preference.

### 4. Nothing Phone (3a), 8 GB/128 GB

- Nothing's India page confirms an 8+128 GB configuration and directs buyers to Flipkart.
- Independent search snippets placed the 8/128 model around **₹24,999** in India, but marketplace results were inconsistent and sometimes showed other 3a variants instead.
- Attractive software experience, camera system, and design; likely a pleasant daily-use phone.
- eSIM and microSD expansion were not confirmed, and the India model should not be assumed to have Gorilla Glass. This is a style/software alternative, not the safest durability-first choice.

### 5. Google Pixel 8a / Pixel 7a, only if discounted

- Google Pixel hardware documentation confirms the Pixel family supports eSIM in supported regions/carriers; it does not imply every regional SKU or carrier combination will activate eSIM.
- Pixel 8a has a strong camera/software proposition and a durable glass front, but has sealed storage, no microSD slot, and older Tensor hardware/battery behaviour than newer midrange alternatives.
- Amazon's live search showed Pixel 7a around **₹29,999**, above budget; Pixel 8a was not reliably listed below ₹25,000. Treat either as a sale-only or open-box/renewed option, not the baseline recommendation.
- For travel, the Pixel is the clearest eSIM-first direction, but only buy from an authorised seller with Indian warranty and verify the exact model number.

### 6. Samsung Galaxy A36 5G, watchlist rather than current pick

- Amazon search showed the 8 GB/128 GB version around **₹26,260**, just above the target budget.
- It is a strong support/durability watchlist candidate because of Samsung's update policy and Victus+ positioning, but it did not meet the strict budget in the observed listing.
- Put it back into contention if a reputable seller brings the 128 GB version below ₹25,000. Verify eSIM and microSD for the Indian SKU rather than assuming either.

## Clarification: built-in glass versus external protection

The earlier report treated built-in Gorilla Glass as too close to a requirement. That was too strict. A phone can be protected with an externally installed tempered-glass screen protector and a raised-edge case. Built-in Gorilla Glass remains a useful durability bonus, but it should not eliminate a phone that otherwise fits well.

Examples of external protection visible in the marketplace research:

- Tempered-glass protectors for Pixel 8a were listed around ₹275 with 18.6K ratings.
- Protectors for Galaxy S24 FE/A36/A56 were listed around ₹277 with 68.9K ratings.
- A case plus a screen protector is normally a ₹750–₹2,000 addition, depending on quality.

## Social-proof refresh

Amazon and Flipkart expose different evidence fields. Amazon commonly shows a rating count plus an approximate recent-purchases signal; Flipkart exposes separate **Ratings** and **Reviews** counts. They should not be merged as if they were the same metric. A large Flipkart count is especially useful for the user's popularity-first requirement, but it still does not replace checking recurring negative reviews.

The strongest current social-proof signals are:

- **Samsung Galaxy S24 FE on Flipkart:** 4.5/5 from **77,805 ratings and 6,539 reviews**, around ₹39,999 for 8/128. This is by far the strongest high-tier popularity signal found.
- **Samsung Galaxy F36 6/128 on Flipkart:** 4.3/5 from **12,879 ratings and 1,142 reviews**, around ₹21,999 in one listing, although that listing was unavailable in the snapshot. This is much stronger social proof than the Amazon listing's 331 rating count.
- **OnePlus Nord CE5 8/128 on Flipkart:** 4.5/5 from **12,664 ratings and 873 reviews**, around ₹22,500, but unavailable in the snapshot. This is the strongest sub-₹25k popularity/performance candidate if stock returns.
- **Samsung Galaxy M17 6/128 on Flipkart:** 4.3/5 from **2,110 ratings and 135 reviews**, around ₹22,880. It is popular, but its social proof is materially smaller than the F36 and Nord CE5 signals.
- **Motorola Edge 60 Fusion on Flipkart:** 4.4/5 from **50,394 ratings and 3,577 reviews**, but ₹37,999 and marked coming soon. The popularity is strong, but the observed price is not budget-friendly.
- **Google Pixel 9a on Flipkart:** the query did not surface a Pixel 9a listing; it surfaced newer Pixel 10a/Pixel 11 products instead. Do not assign a Pixel 9a Flipkart rating from that search.

### What changed the recommendation

The earlier Amazon-only snapshot understated the popularity of several phones. With Flipkart evidence included, the best social-proof-first candidates are now:

1. **Galaxy S24 FE** for the high-budget, most-complete option.
2. **Galaxy F36** for a popular Samsung value option with expandable storage shown on Flipkart.
3. **OnePlus Nord CE5** for a popular performance/battery option under ₹25,000 when available.
4. **Galaxy M17** as a lower-risk value alternative with smaller but still meaningful social proof.

## Marketplace popularity navigation

Both marketplaces provide useful popularity-oriented entry points, but they are not identical:

- Amazon has a dedicated **Amazon Bestsellers** page, described as products popular by sales and updated frequently. Its ranking mixes phones with chargers, accessories, wearables and other electronics, so it needs category filtering.
- Amazon search results also expose **Amazon's Choice**, “Best seller,” rating counts, and recent purchase signals. These are useful discovery clues, not independent quality certifications.
- Flipkart provides a mobiles page with an explicit **Sort By → Popularity** control and exposes separate rating and written-review counts. This is better for the user's social-proof-first workflow because ratings and reviews can be recorded separately.
- Popularity rankings can be distorted by colour, RAM/storage variant, availability, discounts and seller. Record the exact variant and timestamp.

## Interpretation

- **Best social-proof/value direction:** Samsung Galaxy F36 6/128, if it is in stock and the exact seller/warranty are acceptable.
- **Best sub-₹25k performance/popularity direction:** OnePlus Nord CE5 8/128, if it returns to stock; eSIM and microSD remain unconfirmed.
- **Best mainstream value alternative:** Samsung Galaxy M17 6/128, with lower review volume but strong enough evidence and lower risk.
- **Best higher-budget complete balance:** Samsung Galaxy S24 FE, with the strongest popularity signal; confirm eSIM and accept no microSD.
- **Best hardware durability signal:** Motorola Edge 60 Fusion, but only at a verified price and seller; the Flipkart price snapshot was above budget.

## Checks before checkout

1. Confirm exact model number, RAM/storage, SIM tray layout, eSIM support, and whether a microSD card is supported.
2. Confirm Indian warranty, GST invoice, seller reputation, and return/replacement policy.
3. Check current price on both Amazon India and Flipkart; marketplace pages showed inconsistent and rapidly changing prices.
4. Read at least one independent review/benchmark from Notebookcheck, GSMArena, or 91mobiles. Do not rely on manufacturer benchmark comparisons alone.
5. Buy a fitted case with raised bezels and a compatible screen protector; Gorilla Glass is not drop-proof.
