# Mobile purchase decision — corrected shortlist

## Important clarification

A phone does **not** need built-in Gorilla Glass for us to protect it. We can add a tempered-glass screen protector and a proper case. Built-in Gorilla Glass is therefore a bonus, not a hard requirement.

Typical extra budget:

- Tempered glass: **₹250–₹600**
- Good raised-edge case: **₹500–₹1,500**

A protector helps with scratches and minor impacts, but it does not make a phone drop-proof or waterproof.

## Budget conclusion

| Budget | What it realistically buys |
|---|---|
| **₹20k–₹25k** | Popular value phones such as Galaxy F36, OnePlus Nord CE5 when available, or Galaxy M17. Add case and tempered glass. eSIM is the likely compromise. |
| **₹28k–₹32k** | Better durability/performance candidates such as Motorola Edge 60 Fusion or similar. Verify exact SKU and seller carefully. |
| **₹38k–₹42k** | The realistic zone for a highly popular, well-supported, IP-rated phone with a stronger chance of eSIM: Galaxy S24 FE-class devices. Storage is sealed. |
| **₹48k–₹52k** | eSIM-first Pixel 9a-class option with long software support and strong travel suitability. |

### Budget needed to cover almost all major requirements

If the requirements are:

- stable mainstream brand;
- strong user-review volume;
- IP-rated durability;
- eSIM;
- good software/support;
- reliable travel use;
- external tempered glass and case;
- 128 GB or more;

then I would budget **about ₹40,000–₹50,000**. The **₹40,000 Galaxy S24 FE-class tier** is the more sensible value target; the Pixel 9a tier is worth considering if eSIM and clean software are the top priorities.

MicroSD expansion is the one requirement that generally disappears as the phone gets more premium. If microSD is mandatory, remain closer to the Samsung M/F-series value tier and treat eSIM as optional.

## Social-proof-first method

The evidence file now keeps separate rows for Amazon and Flipkart, and records the seller/listing when it was visible. Marketplace counts should not be merged: the same phone can show very different rating totals across platforms and variants.

The TSV also includes a qualitative user-review summary. Examples from the captured snippets:

- Galaxy F36: display and One UI were praised, while battery endurance and fast drain appeared as recurring cautions.
- Galaxy M17: display, everyday performance and value were praised, while heating and battery drain appeared in negative snippets.
- Galaxy S24 FE: camera, processor and battery were praised, while weight was a caution.
- Nord CE5 and Edge 60 Fusion: ratings were captured, but written-review snippets were not captured in the search snapshot; those need a product-page review pass before purchase.

These are directional summaries of visible evidence, not a statistically complete sentiment analysis.

1. **Filter for popularity first:** prefer at least 4.2 stars and 500+ ratings; on Flipkart, record both the Ratings and Reviews counts.
2. **Inspect recurring user complaints:** heating, battery drain, display lines, network problems, camera failures, and service-centre issues matter more than isolated one-star reviews.
3. **Confirm the exact SKU:** eSIM, microSD, RAM, storage, warranty and seller can vary by region and listing.
4. **Then read expert reviews:** use GSMArena, Notebookcheck, 91mobiles, Gadgets 360 and similar sources for battery, camera, thermals, display and update analysis.
5. **Reject vendor-only claims:** manufacturer benchmark numbers are useful context but should not outweigh sustained independent tests and user experience.
6. **Recheck immediately before buying:** marketplace prices, sellers, stock and reviews change.
